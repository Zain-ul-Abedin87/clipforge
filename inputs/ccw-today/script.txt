When the grid goes dark and the shelves get stripped bare, nobody's going to ask what you paid for the gun on your hip. They'll only care whether it fires every single time, and every dollar you didn't waste on a logo is a dollar you spent on ammo and training. Some of the most formidable carry pistols out there cost half what the big names charge, and the rest beat them on capacity, triggers, or sheer toughness. Here's how they stack up.
We're starting at the bottom with what might be the most deceptive pistol on this list, because it rides in a front pocket and nobody sees it coming. Ruger's LCP MAX is just over five inches long and a little over four inches tall, and its slide is barely eight-tenths of an inch wide. It weighs ten point six ounces empty and still holds ten-plus-one rounds of .380 Auto. A Glock 42 holds six. That's nearly double the firepower in a gun that disappears into your jeans. You also get a tritium front sight outlined in white, so you can find it in the dark when it matters. Now, I'll be straight with you. Ruger lists the standard model at about four eighty, and the manual-safety version at about four hundred, so this one doesn't win on price, and I won't pretend it does. It wins on how much it packs into the smallest footprint here. The catch is the caliber. .380 is the floor of serious defense, so your hollow-point choice matters more here than anywhere else. And a gun this light will snap in your hand, so practice with it or don't carry it.
Here's a gun the gun-counter crowd loves to laugh at, right up until they see what comes in the box. The Taurus G3C is six point three inches long, five point one inches tall, and twenty-two ounces empty. It has a three-point-two-inch stainless barrel and twelve-plus-one rounds of nine millimeter. And Taurus ships it with three twelve-round magazines. Plenty of guns that cost far more come with just one. Major retailers list it right around three hundred. SIG's base P365 lists at right around five hundred on SIG's own site, with a ten-round flush magazine. So the G3C gives you two more rounds and two more magazines, and leaves you about two hundred bucks for ammo and a holster. The caveat is Taurus itself. The company earned its bad reputation honestly on older models, so treat this one like a gun you have to prove. Buy it somewhere with a return policy, and put a couple hundred rounds of your carry ammo through it first. It's also one point two inches wide, so it's chunkier than the slimmest micros.
